# Rostam trademark policy

"Rostam" and the Rostam logo are trademarks of RostamLabs.

The Apache License 2.0 covers the **code**. It explicitly does not grant rights
in the project's names or logos (§6). This page says what we do and do not ask
of you, so that neither side has to guess.

## You do not need permission to

- Use, modify, embed, redistribute, sell, or offer Rostam as a service, under
  the terms of the licence. That is the whole point of Apache-2.0 and nothing
  here narrows it.
- Say your product "uses Rostam", is "built on Rostam", or is "compatible with
  Rostam" — accurate, factual statements about what your software does.
- Use the name in articles, talks, benchmarks, comparisons and reviews,
  including critical ones.
- Keep the name in an unmodified redistribution of the project.

## Please ask first before

- Naming your product, company, service, or domain in a way that leads with our
  mark — "Rostam Cloud", "RostamDB", "Rostam Enterprise", `rostam-*.com`. The
  concern is confusion about who stands behind it, not the underlying use.
- Using the logo as your own product's mark, or a modified version of it.
- Distributing a **modified** build under the plain name "Rostam". Ship the fork
  under its own name and describe it as "a fork of Rostam" — this is the normal
  courtesy for any project with a name worth having, and it is what keeps bug
  reports and security advisories pointed at the right code.

Ask at **trademark@rostamlabs.com**. We expect to say yes to most requests; the
policy exists to prevent impersonation, not to police vocabulary.

## Why this exists alongside an open licence

Apache-2.0 deliberately leaves trademarks out, and the split is the useful part:
the licence guarantees the code stays free, and the mark keeps "Rostam" meaning
one identifiable thing. If a modified build could be called plain "Rostam", then
"I'm running Rostam" stops carrying information — for users comparing behaviour,
and for us when someone reports a bug against a build we did not produce.

This policy is applied in that spirit. If a use is honest and unlikely to be
mistaken for an official RostamLabs product, it is almost certainly fine.
